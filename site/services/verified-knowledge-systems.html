Service 01

It cites its sources. And it says when it does not know.

Enterprise retrieval over your own documents, policies and operational data — built so that an answer without a source cannot be produced. Most RAG deployments fail the same way: they answer confidently from general knowledge when retrieval returns nothing useful. The user cannot tell the difference. That failure mode is designed out of this one.

The Problem

Confident answers with nothing underneath.

The failure is not that the system is wrong sometimes. It is that a wrong answer looks exactly like a right one.

Failure 01

Answering From The Model

Retrieval returns three irrelevant chunks. The model answers anyway, from what it learned in training. The answer is plausible, fluent, and about a different company’s policy.

Our fix: retrieval confidence gates the answer. Below threshold, it refuses.

Failure 02

No Source Attached

The answer is correct. Nobody can verify it, because nothing points back to the document it came from. Six months later the policy changes and nobody knows which answers are now stale.

Our fix: every claim carries its source document and section.

Failure 03

Nobody Measures It

The system went live. Accuracy was never sampled. The first evidence of a problem is a customer acting on a wrong answer.

Our fix: monthly sampling, reported — including the errors that reached a user.

The Build

Five to six weeks, including supervision.

The last two weeks are a supervised period where the system runs but is not yet trusted. That is not padding — it is where the accuracy baseline comes from.

Week 1

Corpus audit

What documents exist, how current they are, what contradicts what. Most knowledge bases contain two versions of the same policy. We find those first, because retrieval cannot resolve a contradiction that the corpus contains.

Week 2

Ingestion and chunking

Structure-aware chunking rather than fixed windows. Section headings, tables and policy clauses survive as units. Freshness metadata attached at ingest, so staleness is queryable later.

Week 3–4

Retrieval and refusal

Hybrid retrieval, reranking, and the confidence gate that produces a refusal instead of an answer. The gate is tuned against your own corpus — a threshold that works for legal documents does not work for support tickets.

Week 5–6

Supervised run

The system answers real questions, and every answer is reviewed before it reaches the asker. This produces the accuracy baseline, catches the corpus problems that only surface under real queries, and tunes the refusal rate.

What you receive at the end. A running system, the accuracy baseline from the supervised period, a monthly sampling process your team can operate, and the freshness report that tells you which parts of the corpus have gone stale.

Where It Fits

The teams that buy this first.

The pain is always the same shape: knowledge exists, but only in people’s heads or in documents nobody can find.

01

Support teams answering the same question weekly

Tier-one support where the answer exists in a document but finding it takes longer than asking a colleague. Deflection is measurable from week one.

02

Onboarding that takes six weeks and should take two

New joiners asking questions that are answered in a wiki nobody reads. The system does not replace the wiki — it makes the wiki answerable.

03

Operations running on tribal knowledge

Exception handling, escalation rules and process edge cases that exist only in the memory of whoever has been there longest. The corpus audit usually surfaces that this knowledge was never written down at all — which is itself the finding.

04

Compliance answering the same audit questions annually

Where the evidence exists across four systems and assembling it takes a week. Source attribution is not a nice-to-have here; it is the deliverable.
